Niles — Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 17, 2026

Niles stores and processes everything on the device you install it on. It has no accounts, no sign-in, no analytics, and no servers run by us. Niles does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any personal data. This page explains what the app stores locally, what stays inside your home network, and the few things it can never do.

What Niles does

Niles connects to the Apple Home (HomeKit) homes configured on your device and lets you review proposed changes to them. It also runs a small local-network server so tools and agents you explicitly pair — on the same Wi-Fi network — can read your home's current state and suggest changes. Every change those agents suggest appears inside Niles as a proposal, and nothing is applied to your home until you approve it in the app.

Data stored on your device only

The following live entirely in Niles's own storage on your device and never leave it unless you send them yourself:

  • Your home data. Names, rooms, accessories, services, scenes, triggers, and current state read from your Apple Home.
  • Pairing credentials. One access token per paired agent, stored in the iOS Keychain. Tokens can be revoked from the app at any time.
  • Proposals and activity. Pending and past change proposals, approval decisions, and read-back evidence recorded when changes execute. Proposals expire after 24 hours.
  • Preferences. Settings such as your pairing code and server configuration.

Data that stays inside your home network

The only network traffic Niles generates is on your local network. When serving is on, Niles advertises itself over Bonjour as _niles-mcp._tcp and listens on a local port so paired agents on the same Wi-Fi can connect. Niles makes no requests to any server on the internet, and its local server is not reachable from outside your network by design. Serving runs only while the app is in the foreground, and the first time Niles starts it, iOS shows the standard Local Network permission prompt — your home data is not readable over the network until you allow it and pair a device with a six-digit code.

What paired agents can do

An agent you pair receives one token and can, over your local network:

  • Read the current state of your Apple Home — homes, rooms, accessories, services, scenes, triggers, and related groups.
  • Stage proposed changes, which sit in the Queue inside Niles and do nothing until you approve them there.

A paired agent can never approve or execute a change, never changes your home directly, and never receives your pairing code or your other agents' tokens. You can revoke any agent's token in Settings, which cuts off its access immediately.

What Niles never does

  • No accounts, sign-in, or user profiles.
  • No analytics, tracking, advertising identifiers, or telemetry of any kind.
  • No third-party SDKs that collect data.
  • No sending your home data, proposals, or credentials off your device or off your local network.
  • No reading data from other apps on your device.

HomeKit data

Niles reads and, when you approve a proposal, writes to your Apple Home through Apple's HomeKit framework. That data is governed by Apple's own HomeKit protections; Niles never uses HomeKit data for marketing, advertising, or any form of data mining, and never sends it anywhere.

Children

Niles is a utility for managing your own home. It does not collect data from anyone, including children, because it does not collect data at all.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Niles ever changes what data it handles, this page will be updated before that version ships, and the app's privacy label on the App Store will be updated to match. The app has no update feed precisely because it makes no network requests of its own — so check this page when you update the app.
